# Plain Language

**A smart reader with no background should understand the text in one pass.**

## Rules

1. Lead with the answer. Give enough context to understand the details:
   what happened, who was involved, and where the evidence came from.
2. Use everyday words. Explain needed technical terms, acronyms, and
   internal names when they first appear.
3. Replace working shorthand and invented labels with what they mean.
   Use analogies to explain ideas, not to create new names.
4. Use direct verbs and active voice. Make clear who did what.
5. Keep one point per sentence and one idea per paragraph.
6. Cut words that add no meaning. State ideas directly; avoid double negatives.
7. Avoid repeated rhetorical patterns and forced symmetry. Use dashes
   sparingly; state the logical connection between ideas.
8. Make headings answer the reader's questions. Use lists or tables when
   they make parallel items easier to compare.
9. Give numbers context: what they measure, the comparison, and what the
   difference means. Describe the observed behavior behind scores and labels.
10. State limits and exceptions plainly. Distinguish what the evidence
    shows from what it cannot establish. Explain vague quoted claims.

## Check before delivery

Read twice from an outsider's view:

- First, explain unfamiliar terms and check actors, numbers, and limits.
- Then, catch shorthand and invented labels that still hide meaning.

Read the text aloud in your head. Rewrite anything awkward or unclear.
